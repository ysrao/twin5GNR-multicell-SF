How to read this: Colour = best server (strongest RSRP) at that point. White dots = handoff region, where the best and second-best cells are within 1 CQI of each other — a UE there would normally hand over, and ping-pong is possible. The trigger is the UE-reported CQI (quantised SINR, 0-15): the UE measures SINR on every sector, maps it to CQI, and reports it; the network hands over when a neighbour's CQI beats the serving cell's CQI. SINR here is the real one — serving RSRP against all other sectors' RSRP plus thermal noise — not the old 3 dB-below-serving assumption. Triangle = tower. Dot = UE, with its label. RSRP = EIRP − path loss, modelled. Capacity = bandwidth × log2(1+SINR) × MIMO streams, capped at the MIMO order, modelled, not measured throughput. Doppler = speed × frequency / speed of light, shown per UE. Morphology changes path loss only; it does not change the map shape.

Assumptions, stated: 3GPP TR 38.901 simplified LOS path loss: UMa 28.0 + 22·log10(d) + 20·log10(f); UMi 32.4 + 21·log10(d) + 20·log10(f); RMa 20·log10(d) + 20·log10(f) + 20 approx., d in metres, f in GHz, minimum distance 10 m. Noise −174 dBm/Hz, noise figure 7 dB, SINR is computed per sector from all sectors' RSRP plus thermal noise; the UE reports it as CQI (0-15, ~2 dB steps from -6 dB, stated approx.), and handover is decided on CQI. ICI degradation, calculated (not assumed as a flat factor): ICI ratio = (π·f_D / Δf)² / 3, where f_D is Doppler and Δf is subcarrier spacing (15 kHz at 0.7 GHz, 30 kHz at 3.5 GHz, 60 kHz at 7.125 GHz, 120 kHz at 28 GHz, stated). Effective SINR = 1 / (1/SINR + ICI ratio), for both DL and UL. DL uses tower EIRP and selected MIMO; UL uses UE power 23 dBm and MIMO capped at 2, so UL is usually lower. Antenna pattern (3GPP model) is directional 65° per sector: gain 15 dBi at boresight, falling as 12·(angle/65)², capped at 25 dB down, per 3GPP TR 38.901 simplified pattern. Each tower has 3 sectors at 0°, 120° and 240°. UEs move in straight lines and bounce at the grid edge. This is a teaching / planning sketch, not a site survey.
